2

Load Data

Where the load curves come from, which timestamps become samples, and how loaded and how varied each sample is.

Data source

A Historical database whose series share one grid level. MV and Feeder series populate every connection point (sized by its transformer rating); LV series populate the connection points with a real LV grid attached, summing one series per LV node.

Load level

How heavily loaded the grid is in each sample. Multiplier scales the historical load by a drawn factor; Fraction of P_max rescales each sample to a drawn fraction of P_max, the most demand the grid carries within its voltage and thermal limits (found by a search at the start of the run).

Load shape and noise

Rating sizing takes only each series' shape and sizes each connection point by its transformer rating; Auto uses rating for MV / Feeder databases and the recorded values for LV. Missing reactive power is always filled from a drawn power factor. Noise is log-normal (mean 1); 0 turns each part off.

DER

The generators in each sample: the grid's own, plants you add, or both. Added plants follow the time series of a historical generation database (PV, wind, hydro, ...).

Plants

Aggregated data are the summed output of many plants (e.g. E-REDES' injection by technology): each plant follows the aggregate's shape, normalised by its maximum in the reference year, with its own performance factor and slowly varying random adjustment. Individual data hold one plant per series, used as recorded and rated at its maximum. Each grid draws its own number of plants in a group's range (min = max for a fixed number). Mixed control draws each plant's mode from its type's usual mix.

Set-points

The operating set-points of each device type in every sample. The counts are for the grids selected in step 1.

Busbar

The substation busbar is an ideal source. Load-dependent raises it with the load level, as operators and line-drop compensation do.

Capacitor banks

Fixed: the grid's step. Random: 0 to n steps per bank and sample. Voltage-controlled: each bank switches steps to keep its node inside the band.

Topology & Parameters

Which network each sample is solved on. Each topology variant is a configuration plus an outage set; every sample is solved on every variant unless you limit it below. Islanded parts are kept and flagged, not dropped.

Outages

Exhaustive N-k enumerates every combination of up to k branches out of service; Random draws a number of distinct outage sets of 1 to k branches. Per-sample outages trip DER, take capacitor banks out and bypass regulators.

Reconfiguration

Random radial configurations, drawn uniformly and all distinct. Grids without switches need ties first (Suggest tie lines on the grid page) or All MV lines.

Parameters

Uncertainty in the branch parameters. Length scales R, X and charging together; temperature changes R only (stored R is at 20 °C); a swap replaces a line's cable type by another of its family (underground or overhead), ratings included. The admittance perturbation redraws every branch's R and X within ± sigma.

6

Sampling Design

How the drawn quantities (load level, DER penetration, busbar set-point) pair up across samples, and whether the run should reach a share of samples with violations.

Design

Independent: each quantity is stratified on its own. Latin hypercube: one design with little correlation between them. Factorial: every combination of their bins filled equally (needs at least as many samples as combinations).

Target mix

Solves a pilot first, then oversamples the load levels that produce violations until the share is reached. Every sample gets a design_weight that undoes the oversampling. Needs a load level (step 2).

7

Measurement View

Next to each clean solution, the readings of a meter layout drawn once per run: the substation busbar voltage and the feeder heads always; a share of connection points (voltage, and P, Q through the transformer); every switch and a share of the other cables (P, Q, current); DER above a size. Readings get relative noise and rare outliers from a profile, some go missing, and unmetered connection points get P, Q pseudo-measurements. For a forecasting dataset, use the Window timeline (step 2) and leave the per-sample random settings off.
